DigiLocker Explained: What It Is, How It Works & Similar Apps in India

A detailed guide to DigiLocker, digital documents, verification, security, and related Indian government applications such as UMANG, mAadhaar, mParivahan, e-Shram and the National Academic Depository.

In the past, carrying important documents meant keeping a physical folder filled with certificates, identity cards, licenses, mark sheets and other papers. Losing that folder or damaging an important certificate could create considerable inconvenience.

Digital India has changed the way many of these documents and government services are accessed. One of the most important platforms in this transformation is DigiLocker, a digital document wallet that allows citizens to access, store, share and verify digital documents.

But DigiLocker is not the only digital-government platform available in India. Applications such as UMANG, mAadhaar, mParivahan and e-Shram serve related but different purposes.

Quick idea: DigiLocker is primarily about digital documents, while other government apps specialize in services such as government-service access, Aadhaar, transport, worker registration and academic records.

What Is DigiLocker?

DigiLocker is a digital document wallet developed as part of the Government of India's Digital India programme. It is a flagship initiative of the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).

The basic idea is simple: instead of depending entirely on physical copies of important documents, citizens can access authentic digital documents through a secure online platform.

DigiLocker is described as a secure cloud-based platform for the storage, sharing and verification of documents and certificates.

Depending on the participating issuer, users may be able to access documents such as:

  • Driving Licence
  • Vehicle Registration Certificate
  • Educational certificates
  • Mark sheets
  • Identity-related documents
  • Government certificates
  • Skill and vocational certificates
  • Other documents issued by participating organizations

The availability of a particular document depends on whether the relevant government department, educational institution or organization has integrated with DigiLocker.

Why Was DigiLocker Created?

Traditional government and educational document systems were heavily dependent on paper. This created several problems:

  1. Documents could be lost or damaged.
  2. Citizens had to carry physical copies.
  3. Organizations needed to manually verify documents.
  4. Producing duplicate certificates could take time.
  5. Paper-based administration increased costs.
  6. Fake or altered documents could be difficult to detect.

DigiLocker addresses many of these problems by creating a digital ecosystem connecting citizens, document issuers and organizations that need to verify documents.

A university can issue an academic certificate through the system. The student can access it, and an authorized organization can verify it with the user's consent.

Issued Documents vs Uploaded Documents

One of the most important concepts to understand in DigiLocker is the difference between issued documents and uploaded documents.

Issued Documents

Issued documents are documents made available through DigiLocker by an integrated issuing organization. If an eligible educational board makes a student's certificate available through DigiLocker, that document appears under the user's issued documents.

Issued documents are linked to the original issuing authority.

Uploaded Documents

Users can also upload their own files to DigiLocker, such as scanned certificates, old documents, personal PDFs or supporting documents.

Important distinction: An uploaded file should not automatically be considered equivalent to an officially issued digital document. An issuer-provided document has a stronger connection to the original issuing authority.

Are DigiLocker Documents Legally Valid?

A major advantage of DigiLocker is the legal recognition associated with documents issued through the platform. DigiLocker states that documents issued through its system are deemed to be at par with original physical documents under Rule 9A of the Information Technology (Preservation and Retention of Information by Intermediaries providing Digital Locker Facilities) Rules, 2016.

This does not mean that every random PDF uploaded by a user automatically becomes legally equivalent to an original certificate. The distinction between issuer-provided documents and user-uploaded files remains important.

What Can You Do With DigiLocker?

1. Access Documents Anywhere

Instead of carrying physical copies of every document, users can access available digital documents through their DigiLocker account when required.

2. Fetch Documents From Issuers

Where supported, users can request documents from participating issuers. The document is retrieved from the relevant issuing system rather than simply being uploaded as a photograph or PDF.

3. Share Documents

DigiLocker allows users to share documents with organizations through digital mechanisms. The platform is designed around consent-based document exchange.

4. Verify Documents

Digital documents can contain mechanisms that help organizations verify their authenticity. For example, certain transport documents support QR-based and digital-signature verification.

Is DigiLocker Safe?

Security is important because DigiLocker can contain sensitive personal documents. DigiLocker's stated security measures include:

  • OTP-based authentication
  • Multifactor authentication
  • Encryption for data transmission
  • Secure hosting facilities
  • Data redundancy
  • Automatic logout after inactivity
  • Security audits
  • Consent-based document sharing

Users should still follow normal digital-security practices. Never share an OTP, use strong account security, avoid untrusted devices and be cautious about phishing messages.

Applications Similar to DigiLocker in India

DigiLocker exists within a larger ecosystem of Indian digital-government platforms. Some overlap with DigiLocker, while others serve completely different purposes.

1. UMANG

UMANG, or Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance, is one of the closest complementary platforms to DigiLocker.

While DigiLocker focuses heavily on documents, UMANG focuses on bringing many government services together in one place.

Examples of service areas include:

  • Government schemes
  • Utility services
  • State government services
  • EPFO-related services
  • Pension-related services
  • Healthcare
  • Education
  • Transportation
  • Grievances
  • Other government services
DigiLocker = Documents
UMANG = Government Services

UMANG and DigiLocker are not competing applications in the strict sense. They can work together, and UMANG provides access to DigiLocker-related services.

2. mAadhaar

mAadhaar is the official mobile application associated with India's Aadhaar ecosystem and is operated by the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI).

Depending on the supported service, users can use mAadhaar for functions such as:

  • Accessing Aadhaar information
  • Downloading Aadhaar
  • Checking authentication history
  • Locking and unlocking biometrics
  • Reviewing Aadhaar update history
  • Updating address through supported processes
  • Ordering a PVC Aadhaar card

DigiLocker: multiple document types from different issuers.
mAadhaar: primarily Aadhaar-related identity services.

3. mParivahan

mParivahan is an important application in India's digital transport ecosystem. Its focus is transportation-related information and services.

It is particularly relevant to:

  • Driving licences
  • Vehicle registration
  • Vehicle-related information
  • Transport services

There is also integration between the transport ecosystem and DigiLocker for accessing digital driving licences and registration certificates.

DigiLocker: broad digital document ecosystem.
mParivahan: transport-focused digital services.

4. e-Shram

e-Shram is a Government of India digital platform developed by the Ministry of Labour & Employment to create a National Database of Unorganised Workers.

The platform is intended to help unorganised workers access welfare and social-security related services more efficiently.

The database includes information such as:

  • Name
  • Occupation
  • Address
  • Occupation type
  • Educational qualification
  • Skills

The platform covers categories including migrant workers, construction workers, gig workers and platform workers.

DigiLocker: digital document management and verification.
e-Shram: worker registration and social-security ecosystem.

5. National Academic Depository

The National Academic Depository, or NAD, is connected with DigiLocker and focuses on digital academic awards.

Educational institutions can issue academic awards digitally, while students can access and share them. Employers, universities and other authorized organizations can verify academic awards with appropriate consent.

This is particularly useful because academic certificates are among the documents people frequently need throughout their education and careers.

Limitations and Things Users Should Know

A document generally needs support from an integrated issuer to appear as an issued document.

Uploaded Files Are Different

Uploading a scanned document does not necessarily give it the same status as an officially issued digital document.

Internet or Device Access May Be Required

In many situations, accessing a digital document requires a functioning device and appropriate connectivity.

Keep Your Account Secure

Your DigiLocker account may contain sensitive personal information, so protecting your credentials and OTPs is essential.

Acceptance Can Depend on the Context

Although issued DigiLocker documents have legal recognition under applicable rules, users should still follow the specific requirements of the organization or service they are dealing with.
